% ── Abstract ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We present an end-to-end computational pipeline for designing low-energy
Earth--Moon transfers by exploiting the invariant manifold structure of
periodic halo orbits near the collinear libration point $L_1$ of the Circular
Restricted Three-Body Problem (CR3BP).
%
Starting from a third-order Richardson analytic initial guess, a
single-shooting differential correction scheme is used to compute numerically
exact halo orbits; pseudo-arclength continuation then traces the
$L_1$ northern halo family.
%
Stable and unstable manifold tubes are generated by perturbing along the
eigenvectors of the monodromy matrix and integrating forward or backward in
time.
%
Manifold-to-manifold transfers are identified by intersecting the respective
tubes on a Poincaré section defined by the $y=0$ plane in the rotating frame,
and the connecting impulsive $\Delta v$ is computed at the section crossing.
%
A simplified two-body-based estimate provides the Trans-Lunar Injection burn
from a circular 200\,km low Earth orbit to the manifold insertion point.
%
An optional continuous low-thrust leg, modeled as constant tangential thrust,
is incorporated via a single-shooting targeting method to connect the parking
orbit to the manifold.
%
For a representative $L_1$ halo orbit with out-of-plane amplitude
$\Az \approx 0.05$ (non-dimensional, $\approx 19{,}200$\,km), the total
transfer $\Delta v$ from LEO is found to be competitive with classical
bi-elliptic transfers, consistent with the ballistic-capture corridor
identified in prior work.
%
The pipeline is released as open-source Python software and is intended as a
modular foundation for future low-thrust trajectory optimization and solar sail
guidance studies.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords:}
CR3BP, halo orbits, invariant manifolds, Poincaré section,
low-energy transfer, differential correction, Earth--Moon.
\end{abstract}

% ── 1. Introduction ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The design of fuel-efficient trajectories from Earth to the Moon (or to lunar
orbit) has been a central problem in astrodynamics since the Apollo era.
Classical Hohmann transfers and three-burn bi-elliptic sequences are easy to
analyze but miss the dynamical richness available when the Sun's gravity is
included or when three-body resonances are exploited.
%
The foundational insight, developed independently by
\citet{Conley1968} and elaborated in the mission-design context by
\citet{Belbruno1992}, is that the {\em invariant manifolds} of periodic orbits
near the collinear libration points provide natural low-energy pathways between
the Earth--Moon neighborhood and low Earth orbit.
These \emph{manifold highways} have been used in several real missions:
the ARTEMIS mission's transfer to the Earth--Moon $L_1$ point
\citep{Broschart2014,Folta2012} and the Genesis solar-wind sample return
\citep{Lo1998}.

The starting point for all such analyses is the Circular Restricted Three-Body
Problem (CR3BP), which models the motion of a spacecraft of negligible mass
under the gravitational influence of two massive bodies (Earth and Moon) in
their mutual circular orbit \citep{Szebehely1967}.
The CR3BP has five equilibrium (Lagrange) points; the collinear points $L_1$
(between Earth and Moon) and $L_2$ (beyond the Moon) are of particular
interest for mission design because they admit large families of periodic
orbits (halo and Lyapunov families) that are both reachable with modest
$\Delta v$ and close to the Moon.

\subsection*{Objectives and Contributions}

This paper makes the following contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A self-contained Python implementation of the CR3BP halo orbit
    computation pipeline, from Richardson's third-order analytic initial guess
    \citep{Richardson1980} through differential correction
    \citep{Howell1984} and pseudo-arclength continuation.
  \item An invariant manifold generation module based on monodromy matrix
    eigenvector perturbation, with efficient Poincaré section crossing detection.
  \item A manifold intersection search algorithm on the $y=0$ Poincaré section,
    returning the best manifold-to-manifold $\Delta v$ candidates.
  \item A full transfer $\Delta v$ budget, combining a two-body TLI estimate
    with the manifold-stitching cost.
  \item An optional continuous low-thrust leg connecting LEO to the manifold
    insertion point, solved by a shooting method.
\end{enumerate}

All code is available at \url{https://github.com/Bishwaswarup/cr3bp-manifold-transfer}
and archived on Zenodo at \url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.[DOI]}.

% ── 2. Background: CR3BP ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{The Circular Restricted Three-Body Problem}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Non-dimensionalization}

The standard non-dimensionalization uses the Earth--Moon distance
$L^* = 384{,}400$\,km as the length scale and the inverse mean motion
$T^* = 1/n$ (where $n$ is the Moon's mean motion) as the time scale:
\begin{equation}
  n = \sqrt{\frac{G(m_\oplus + m_{\rm Moon})}{(L^*)^3}}
  \approx \SI{2.661e-6}{\radian\per\second},
  \qquad
  T^* = \frac{1}{n} \approx \SI{375700}{\second} \approx 4.34~\text{days}.
\end{equation}
The characteristic velocity is $V^* = L^*/T^* \approx 1.0232$\,km/s.
The mass parameter is
\begin{equation}
  \mu = \frac{m_{\rm Moon}}{m_\oplus + m_{\rm Moon}} \approx 0.012151.
\end{equation}
In non-dimensional units, the Earth is at $(-\mu,\,0,\,0)$ and the Moon at
$(1-\mu,\,0,\,0)$ in the rotating frame.

\subsection{Equations of Motion}

In the synodic (rotating) reference frame, the equations of motion are
\citep{Szebehely1967,KoonLoMarsdenRoss2008}:
\begin{align}
  \ddot{x} - 2\dot{y} &= \frac{\partial \Omega}{\partial x}, \label{eq:eom_x}\\
  \ddot{y} + 2\dot{x} &= \frac{\partial \Omega}{\partial y}, \label{eq:eom_y}\\
  \ddot{z}             &= \frac{\partial \Omega}{\partial z}, \label{eq:eom_z}
\end{align}
where the pseudo-potential $\Omega$ is
\begin{equation}
  \Omega(x,y,z) = \frac{1}{2}(x^2+y^2)
    + \frac{1-\mu}{r_1} + \frac{\mu}{r_2}
    + \frac{1}{2}\mu(1-\mu),
\end{equation}
with $r_1 = \|(x+\mu,\,y,\,z)\|$ and $r_2 = \|(x-1+\mu,\,y,\,z)\|$ the
distances from Earth and Moon, respectively.

\subsection{Jacobi Integral}

The only known first integral of the CR3BP is the Jacobi constant
\begin{equation}
  C = 2\Omega - v^2,\qquad v^2 = \dot{x}^2+\dot{y}^2+\dot{z}^2.
  \label{eq:jacobi}
\end{equation}
Conservation of $C$ provides a key sanity check for numerical integrations
and constrains the accessible regions of phase space (Hill's regions).

\subsection{Lagrange Points and Linear Stability}

The five Lagrange equilibria are found by setting $\dot{\bfx} = \bm{0}$ in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:eom_x}--\eqref{eq:eom_z}.
The collinear points $L_1$--$L_3$ are on the $x$-axis; $L_4$ and $L_5$ form
equilateral triangles with the primaries.
Linear stability analysis of $L_1$ shows one pair of purely imaginary
eigenvalues $\pm i\,\omega_p$ (center) and one pair of real eigenvalues
$\pm \lambda_1$ (saddle), giving the system center$\times$saddle topology that
underlies the existence of halo orbits and their hyperbolic manifolds.

% ── 3. Halo Orbit Computation ─────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Halo Orbit Computation}
\label{sec:halos}

\subsection{Richardson Third-Order Analytic Guess}
\label{ssec:richardson}

\citet{Richardson1980} derived a third-order Lindstedt--Poincaré expansion for
periodic orbits about the collinear libration points.
Given an out-of-plane amplitude $\Az$, the expansion provides
analytic expressions for the in-plane amplitude $A_x$ and the period
$T_0 \approx 2\pi/\lambda_1$ that avoid secular terms at third order,
yielding an initial state $(x_0,\,0,\,z_0,\,0,\,\dot{y}_0,\,0)$ (at the
$y=0$ crossing) accurate to $\mathcal{O}(A^3)$ in the amplitudes.

\subsection{Differential Correction}
\label{ssec:diffcorr}

The Richardson guess satisfies the periodicity conditions only approximately.
Single-shooting differential correction refines it by treating
$\mathbf{p} = (z_0,\,\dot{y}_0,\,T/2)$ as free variables and requiring
\begin{equation}
  \bm{F}(\mathbf{p}) =
  \begin{pmatrix} \dot{x}(T/2) \\ \dot{z}(T/2) \end{pmatrix} = \bm{0},
  \label{eq:dc_constraint}
\end{equation}
which enforces a symmetric (mirror-image) periodic orbit about the $xz$-plane.
The Newton update is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\mathbf{p} = -\mathbf{J}^+\,\bm{F}(\mathbf{p}),
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{J}^+ = \mathbf{J}^T(\mathbf{J}\mathbf{J}^T)^{-1}$ is the
Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse of the $2\times 3$ constraint Jacobian, and
$\mathbf{J}$ is computed analytically from the state transition matrix (STM)
$\bm{\Phi}(T/2,\,0)$.

\subsection{State Transition Matrix}

The STM $\bm{\Phi}(t,\,t_0)$ satisfies the variational equation
\begin{equation}
  \dot{\bm{\Phi}} = A(t)\,\bm{\Phi},\qquad \bm{\Phi}(t_0,t_0) = \mathbf{I}_6,
  \label{eq:stm_ode}
\end{equation}
where $A(t)$ is the $6\times 6$ Jacobian of the CR3BP vector field evaluated
along the reference trajectory.
We integrate Eq.~\eqref{eq:stm_ode} simultaneously with the equations of
motion to obtain the STM at any desired time.

\subsection{Pseudo-Arclength Continuation}
\label{ssec:continuation}

Once a single halo orbit is converged, the family is traced using
pseudo-arclength continuation \citep{Keller1977}.
A predictor step is taken along the tangent $\hat{\mathbf{t}}$ to the
solution manifold:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{p}^{k+1}_{\rm pred} = \mathbf{p}^k + \Delta s\,\hat{\mathbf{t}}^k,
\end{equation}
where $\Delta s$ is the arclength step size.
The corrector then solves the augmented system
\begin{equation}
  \begin{pmatrix} \bm{F}(\mathbf{p}) \\ (\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^k)^T\hat{\mathbf{t}}^k - \Delta s \end{pmatrix}
  = \bm{0},
\end{equation}
preventing the Newton iteration from folding back onto the previous solution.

% ── 4. Invariant Manifolds ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Invariant Manifold Tubes}
\label{sec:manifolds}

\subsection{Monodromy Matrix}

The monodromy matrix is the STM evaluated after one full period:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{M} = \bm{\Phi}(T,\,0).
\end{equation}
For a halo orbit in the CR3BP, the eigenspectrum of $\mathbf{M}$ contains
one eigenvalue $\lambda > 1$ (unstable), its reciprocal $1/\lambda$ (stable),
a repeated unit eigenvalue (neutral, tangent to the orbit family), and a
complex-conjugate pair $e^{\pm i\theta}$ (center) \citep{KoonLoMarsdenRoss2008}.

\subsection{Manifold Generation}

The global unstable manifold $W^u$ is tangent to the unstable eigenvector
$\mathbf{v}^u$ of $\mathbf{M}$ at the fiducial point.
At any other point $\bfx(t_i)$ on the orbit, the manifold direction is given by
the STM-transported eigenvector:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{v}^u(t_i) = \bm{\Phi}(t_i,0)\,\mathbf{v}^u(0) \Big/ \big\|\bm{\Phi}(t_i,0)\,\mathbf{v}^u(0)\big\|.
\end{equation}
A perturbed initial condition $\bfx_\pm(t_i) = \bfx(t_i) \pm \varepsilon\,\mathbf{v}^u(t_i)$
(with $\varepsilon = 10^{-6}$ in our implementation) is then integrated forward
for $\sim 3T$ to produce one trajectory of the unstable manifold tube
$W^u_{\rm loc}$.
The stable manifold is computed analogously using $\mathbf{v}^s$ and backward
integration.

\begin{remark}
The perturbation size $\varepsilon$ must be small enough that the linear
approximation is valid (the trajectory stays close to the manifold) but large
enough to avoid excessive integration time before the trajectory separates
from the orbit.  We find $\varepsilon = 10^{-6}$ to be a robust choice for
the Earth--Moon system.
\end{remark}

% ── 5. Poincaré Section and Intersection Search ───────────────────────────────
\section{Poincaré Section and Intersection Search}
\label{sec:poincare}

\subsection{Section Definition}

We use the $y=0$ surface (the $xz$-plane in the rotating frame) as the
Poincaré section $\Sigma$.
Each manifold trajectory is intersected with $\Sigma$ by detecting sign changes
in the $y$-coordinate between successive integration steps and refining the
crossing point by linear interpolation.

\subsection{Intersection Criterion}

A transfer is possible when the unstable manifold of the departure orbit and
the stable manifold of the arrival orbit intersect transversally on $\Sigma$.
For the single-orbit case (departure = arrival), this corresponds to a
homoclinic connection; for two different orbits, a heteroclinic connection.

Given section crossing states $\mathbf{s}_d \in W^u$ and $\mathbf{s}_a \in W^s$,
we define the position mismatch
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{\rm pos} = \sqrt{(x_d-x_a)^2 + (z_d-z_a)^2}
\end{equation}
and the stitching $\Delta v$
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{v}_a - \mathbf{v}_d,\qquad
  \dv = |\Delta\mathbf{v}| \cdot V^*.
  \label{eq:dv_stitch}
\end{equation}
The best transfer candidate minimizes $(\delta_{\rm pos},\, \dv)$ jointly,
with position mismatch as the primary sorting key.

% ── 6. Transfer Design and ΔV Budget ─────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Transfer Design and $\Delta v$ Budget}
\label{sec:transfer}

\subsection{Trans-Lunar Injection (TLI)}

The departure from a circular low Earth orbit (LEO) of altitude $h$ requires
a perigee burn to enter a transfer ellipse tangent to the manifold insertion
point.
Using the two-body approximation (valid since the spacecraft is deep inside the
Earth's sphere of influence), the TLI cost is
\begin{equation}
  \dv_1 = \left| v_{\rm peri} - v_{\rm circ}(r_{\rm LEO}) \right|,\qquad
  \dv_2 = \left| v_{\rm circ}(r_{\rm ins}) - v_{\rm apo} \right|,
\end{equation}
where $r_{\rm ins}$ is the non-dimensional distance from Earth to the manifold
insertion point, and $v_{\rm peri}$, $v_{\rm apo}$ are the perigee and apogee
speeds of the Hohmann transfer ellipse.

\subsection{Optional Low-Thrust Leg}

As an alternative to impulsive TLI, a continuous low-thrust arc with constant
tangential thrust acceleration $a_T$ can connect the parking orbit to the
manifold insertion point.
The equations of motion become
\begin{equation}
  \ddot{\bfx} + \text{(Coriolis + gravity)} = a_T\,\hat{\mathbf{v}},
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\mathbf{v}} = \mathbf{v}/|\mathbf{v}|$ is the unit velocity vector
(tangential thrust law).
A single-shooting targeting method nulls the position and velocity error at
the manifold insertion point, with free variables $(\dot{x}_0,\,\dot{y}_0,\,\dot{z}_0,\,T_{\rm thrust})$.

\subsection{Full Budget}

The total transfer $\Delta v$ is
\begin{equation}
  \dv_{\rm total} = \dv_1 + \dv_2 + \dv_{\rm stitch},
  \label{eq:dv_total}
\end{equation}
where $\dv_{\rm stitch}$ is the Poincaré patch burn from Eq.~\eqref{eq:dv_stitch}.
Arrival at the halo orbit from the stable manifold is ballistic (no burn needed
at the destination).

% ── 8. Conclusion ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented a complete, open-source pipeline for manifold-assisted
Earth--Moon transfer design in the CR3BP.
The pipeline successfully generates halo orbit families at $L_1$, computes
their stable and unstable invariant manifold tubes, identifies manifold
intersections on a Poincaré section, and assembles a full $\Delta v$ budget.
The resulting transfers are competitive with classical bi-elliptic trajectories
and require only a small stitching burn at the Poincaré section.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The current analysis uses the autonomous CR3BP and a circular Earth--Moon
orbit; real mission design requires ephemeris-based propagation, solar
perturbations, and a full optimal-control formulation for the low-thrust leg.
The TLI estimate uses a two-body approximation; a full CR3BP shooting method
would reduce this error.

\paragraph{Future Work.}
Direct extensions include:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Optimal low-thrust trajectory design}: replacing the tangential
    steering law with an indirect or direct optimization (e.g.,
    Pontryagin maximum principle or direct transcription) to minimize
    propellant consumption or transfer time.
  \item \textbf{Solar sail control}: applying the manifold-tube framework to
    solar sail trajectories, where the sail cone angle replaces the thrust
    direction as the control variable — directly relevant to ongoing solar
    sail guidance and navigation research \citep{Chujo2020}.
  \item \textbf{Ephemeris model}: upgrading to the JPL DE440 ephemeris to
    assess the sensitivity of the manifold intersection to lunar orbit
    eccentricity and solar perturbations.
\end{itemize}
